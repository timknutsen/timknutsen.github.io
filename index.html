Salmonid genomics, bioinformatics and applied AI

Tim Martin Knutsen

I work on salmonid genomics and bioinformatics at AquaGen, mostly where biological data has to become useful in a breeding program.

My interests are practical rather than tidy. I work with genomic data, image-based phenotyping, and the data infrastructure needed to make analyses repeatable. I also use AI as a tool for coding, analysis, writing, and technical work, and I follow LLMs, agents, Codex, Claude Code, and efficient AI use closely because they are becoming part of the scientific workflow.

Research

I am most interested in methods and tools that make biological data more useful. For me, the common thread is not one model class or one trait, but whether the work improves how we interpret salmonid biology and how quickly we can turn data into something reliable.

Salmonid genomics.

I work with genomic data in Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout, especially where the result needs to support breeding, health, robustness, or better biological interpretation.

Bioinformatics.

I care about the practical side of bioinformatics: reproducible analysis, data handling, automation, and workflows that are transparent enough to be reused and questioned.

Computer vision.

Image-based phenotyping is an obvious place where aquaculture can get more from the data it already produces. I am interested in using computer vision to measure fish and extract biological information from images and video.

AI for scientific work.

I use LLMs and coding agents as practical tools, not as decoration. They are already useful for programming, analysis, writing, and learning, and they will change how small expert teams work.

PhD Thesis

Genomics of bovine milk fat composition. Doctoral thesis, Norwegian University of Life Sciences, 2018. Series: PhD Thesis;2018:19.

The thesis examined mutations affecting fatty acid composition in Norwegian Red cattle milk, with emphasis on de novo synthesized fatty acids and the major milk fatty acids palmitic acid and oleic acid.
