Quantitative genetics and genomics

Tim Martin Knutsen

Senior scientist at AquaGen AS, Trondheim, working on genetic analysis, salmonid genomics, recombination, and breeding data systems.

I use molecular and quantitative genetic data to understand how useful variation is generated, maintained, and translated into applied breeding decisions. My current work is centered on Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout, with earlier work on bovine milk fat composition.

Research

My research interests are pragmatic: I am interested in genetic variation when it can improve inference, strengthen breeding decisions, or make biological data more useful in production settings.

Salmonid genomics.

Genome-wide markers and molecular data are used to improve interpretation of traits relevant to aquaculture breeding, including health, robustness, growth, and sea lice resistance.

Meiotic recombination.

Recombination rate and crossover distribution affect how genetic variation is reshuffled between generations. I work on these questions in Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout.

Breeding data systems.

Large breeding and molecular data sets place heavy demands on traceability. I build and use workflows that make analyses reproducible enough for repeated scientific and applied use.

Functional variation.

Earlier work focused on variants affecting bovine milk fat composition, including fatty acids with relevance for milk quality, nutrition, and dairy production traits.

PhD Thesis

Genomics of bovine milk fat composition. Doctoral thesis, Norwegian University of Life Sciences, 2018. Series: PhD Thesis;2018:19.

The thesis examined mutations affecting fatty acid composition in Norwegian Red cattle milk, with emphasis on de novo synthesized fatty acids and the major milk fatty acids palmitic acid and oleic acid.
